
Wireless communication is now a common phenomenon. Many of us have WiFi internet connections in our offices and homes. But Bluetooth devices communicate directly with each other, rather than sending traffic through an in-between device such as a wireless router. This makes life very convenient and keeps power use extremely low, improving [battery](https://electronics.howstuffworks.com/everyday-tech/battery.htm) life.

There are two types of Bluetooth technology as of 2020: Bluetooth Low Energy (LE) and Bluetooth Classic (more formally known as Bluetooth Basic Rate/Enhanced Data Rate, or BR/EDR) [source: [Bluetooth SIG](https://www.bluetooth.com/learn-about-bluetooth/bluetooth-technology/radio-versions/)]. Both operate using the same frequency band, but Bluetooth LE is the more popular option, by far. It needs much less energy to operate and can also be used for broadcast or mesh networks in addition to allowing communication over point-to-point connections between two devices.

The classic Bluetooth technology can deliver a slightly higher data rate than Bluetooth LE (3 Mbs compared to either 1Mbs or 2 Mbs) but can only be used for communication directly between two devices using point-to-point connections. Each of the two types of Bluetooth technology has its particular strengths and manufacturers adopt the version that best fits the needs of their product.


### Why Is It Called Bluetooth?

Harald Bluetooth was king of Denmark in the late 900s. He managed to unite Denmark and part of Norway into a single kingdom, then introduced Christianity into Denmark. He left a large monument, the Jelling rune stone, in memory of his parents. He was killed in 986 during a battle with his son, Svend Forkbeard. Choosing this name for the standard indicates how important companies from the Nordic region (nations including Denmark, Sweden, Norway and Finland) are to the communications industry, even if it says little about the way the technology works.

Used for devices such as wireless headphones, speakers, and car audio systems. It is designed for continuous data exchange, such as streaming audio.

- Connections can use encryption to protect transmitted data.

#### Bluetooth LE (Low Energy)

Designed for low power consumption, making it suitable for fitness trackers, smartwatches, sensors, and other battery-powered devices.

- Devices can advertise their presence using small radio messages.

- A smartphone can scan for those messages.

- Pairing is optional for some applications, depending on their security requirements.


Remember: Classic Bluetooth is commonly used for continuous communication, while Bluetooth LE is optimized for low energy use. Both can support secure communication.


### What are pairing and advertising?

These are two different processes.

#### Pairing

Two devices establish a security relationship by exchanging or creating security keys.

Example: You pair your headphones with your phone so they can connect securely in the future.

#### Advertising

A Bluetooth LE device broadcasts small messages announcing its presence or capabilities.

Example: Your fitness tracker advertises itself, and your phone scans for nearby trackers.

Advertising does not automatically mean the devices are paired or that their data is encrypted. Those depend on the connection and security requirements.

### What determines Bluetooth range?

Bluetooth does not have one fixed range. It depends on several factors.

|Factor|Simple meaning|
|---|---|
|Radio spectrum|The frequencies used to communicate|
|Physical layer (PHY)|How data is transmitted over radio|
|Receiver sensitivity|How weak a signal the receiver can still understand|
|Transmission power|How strongly the device transmits|
|Antenna gain|How effectively the antenna sends or receives radio energy|
|Path loss|How much the signal weakens as it travels|

For example, a Bluetooth device with higher transmission power and a good antenna may work farther away. However, higher transmission power generally consumes more battery.

Forward error correction (FEC) adds extra information to transmitted data so the receiver can recover some corrupted or missing information. It can improve communication at weak signal levels without increasing transmission power. The actual range improvement depends on the Bluetooth implementation and environment.


### How does Bluetooth security work?

Bluetooth uses several security mechanisms. The important ones are:

#### Pairing and bonding

Pairing establishes security credentials. Bonding means retaining the relevant keys so devices can recognize and reconnect to each other later.

#### Encryption

Encryption makes transmitted data unreadable to someone who does not possess the necessary key.

#### Authentication

Helps verify that the device or person involved in pairing is the intended one. Some pairing methods provide stronger protection against someone intercepting the setup process than others.

#### Privacy

Supported Bluetooth features can use changing addresses to make it harder to track a device by its radio address alone.

### Is pairing alone enough to make Bluetooth secure?

No. Pairing does not guarantee that every connection is encrypted or that every pairing method is equally secure. Security depends on the Bluetooth version, device implementation, pairing method, and application.

For example, when pairing a phone with a car, comparing a matching code on both devices is generally safer than blindly accepting an unexpected pairing request.


## What does discoverable mode mean?

- Discoverable: Other devices scanning nearby may be able to find your device.

- Non-discoverable: Your device generally does not respond to discovery in the same way, making it harder to find.

- Bluetooth off: The device stops using Bluetooth radio communication, subject to device-specific behavior.


Non-discoverable mode is not a complete security measure. A device may still communicate with devices it already knows, depending on its behavior and settings.
