
## What is edge computing?

Edge computing is a [distributed computing](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/distributed-computing) framework that brings enterprise applications closer to [data](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data) sources, such as [Internet of Things (IoT)](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/internet-of-things) devices or local edge servers.

Sending all device-generated data to a centralized [data center](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-centers) or to the cloud creates bandwidth and [latency](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/latency) issues. Edge computing solves this issue by processing and analyzing data at the origin point, enabling faster and more comprehensive data analysis, such as through mobile edge computing on [5G](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/5g) networks. This move creates the opportunity for greater insights, quicker response times and better [customer experiences](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/customer-experience).


Instead of your device talking to a server across the country, it talks to a mini-server right next to it—at the "edge" of the network.

### The Problem It Solves: The Long Journey to the Cloud

In a traditional setup, every time an Internet of Things (IoT) device gathers data, that information travels a long journey:

1. **The Device** (e.g., a security camera) captures footage.
2. The data travels over the internet to a **Central Cloud Server** located states away.
3. The cloud processes the data (e.g., detects an intruder) and sends a command back.
4. The camera finally acts.

This journey causes **latency** (delay), consumes massive **network bandwidth**, and relies completely on a continuous internet connection. Edge computing cuts this journey out entirely.

### How Edge Computing Works

Edge computing places small, localized hardware units—like micro-data centers, smart routers, or specialized processors—directly on-site.

- **Instant Decisions:** The local edge device processes the critical data instantly.
- **Smart Uploading:** The edge device only sends a tiny, summarized fraction of that data back to the central cloud for long-term storage or deep analytics.

## Real-World Examples

- **Self-Driving Cars:** A autonomous vehicle traveling at 60 mph cannot afford to wait 200 milliseconds for a cloud server to tell it to brake for a pedestrian. The car itself acts as an edge computer, processing camera and LiDAR data instantly to make split-second decisions.
- **Smart Manufacturing & Factories:** Sensors on a factory assembly line monitor machine health. An edge server right on the factory floor can detect a dangerous vibration spike and shut the machine down in microseconds, preventing a catastrophic failure before the cloud would even register the alert.
- **Smart Home Devices:** When you say a command to a smart speaker, older models had to send your voice recording to the cloud to understand it. Modern smart hubs use edge processing to decode common voice commands locally, turning on your lights instantly even if your internet goes down.
- **Retail & Security:** Security cameras at a grocery store use edge AI to analyze video feeds locally for checkout lines or theft detection, saving the store from paying massive monthly cloud fees to stream thousands of hours of high-definition video over the internet.

## Why we need it

1. **It's way faster (Low Latency):** There is no waiting for data to travel across the internet. This is a matter of life and death for things like **self-driving cars**, which need to brake instantly for a pedestrian.
2. **It saves internet data (Bandwidth):** A security camera doesn't need to stream 24 hours of empty hallway video to the cloud. With edge computing, the camera is smart enough to only send a video clip when it actually sees someone walking by.
3. **It works offline:** A smart factory or oil rig out in the ocean can keep running and making decisions even if the internet completely cuts out.
